How the calculation works

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit, rounded up.

Contribution per unit is selling price minus variable cost. With £1,000 in fixed costs, a £20 price and £12 variable cost, break-even is 125 units and £2,500 in revenue.

When to use the break-even calculator

Break-even is the point where sales cover all of your costs: nothing lost, nothing made. Knowing it tells you how many units a product, a market stall or a month of trading needs to sell before it starts making a profit.

Fixed costs stay the same whatever you sell (for example a pitch fee or software). Variable costs rise with each unit (materials, packaging, payment fees).

Worked example

  1. Fixed costs: £450 for a craft fair weekend.
  2. Selling price: £14 per candle; variable cost: £5.50 per candle.
  3. Contribution per unit: £14 − £5.50 = £8.50.
  4. Break-even: £450 ÷ £8.50 = 52.9, rounded up to 53 candles, or £742 in sales.

Common questions

What counts as a variable cost?

Anything you spend for each unit sold: materials, packaging, labels, card fees and delivery you pay for.

Why is the result rounded up?

You cannot sell part of a unit, and rounding down would leave some costs uncovered.

How can I lower my break-even point?

Reduce fixed costs, reduce the variable cost per unit, or raise the price. The wholesale price calculator shows the effect of a margin target.
