How the calculation works

Reorder point = average daily demand × lead time in days + safety stock.

The result is rounded up to the next whole unit. For example, 12 units a day, 5 days of lead time and 10 units of safety stock give a reorder point of 70 units.

When to use the reorder point calculator

The reorder point is the stock level at which you place your next order, so new stock arrives before you run out. It covers the demand expected while you wait for the supplier, plus a buffer of safety stock for busier days or late deliveries.

Recalculate it when sales change with the season or when a supplier’s lead time changes.

Worked example

  1. Average daily demand: 8 units.
  2. Supplier lead time: 6 days, so demand during the lead time is 8 × 6 = 48 units.
  3. Safety stock: 15 units.
  4. Reorder point: 48 + 15 = 63 units. When stock falls to 63, place the next order.

Common questions

How much safety stock should I keep?

Enough to cover the extra demand or delay you see on a bad week. Start with a few days of sales for items that matter most, then adjust from real stock records.

What if the supplier sells in cases?

Use the reorder point to decide when to order, then round the quantity up to whole cases. The retail reorder guide shows a worked example.

How is this different from EOQ?

The reorder point tells you when to order; economic order quantity (EOQ) estimates how much to order each time. The free EOQ calculator covers the second question.
