How to set retail reorder levels and supplier case quantities

A useful ordering list tells you how many units the shop needs, how many supplier cases to buy and what the order is likely to cost. Those are related figures, but they are not the same thing.

By the InventoryOS team · 27 October 2026

On this page

  1. Separate the trigger from the target
  2. Subtract deliveries that are already on the way
  3. Round the shortage into supplier packs
  4. Compare cost per unit, not just the case price
  5. Investigate negative stock instead of ignoring it
  6. Review the levels after the shop has traded

Separate the trigger from the target

The minimum stock level is the point at which a product needs attention. The target level is the amount you want to reach after replenishment. Set both around the product’s sales pattern, supplier lead time and the space you have available.

For example, a shop might want a water line flagged when it reaches 24 units, with a target of 60. At 18 units on hand, the initial gap to the target is 42. Treat these numbers as a worked example, not a recommended threshold for every shop.

Subtract deliveries that are already on the way

Ordering another 42 units would overstate the need if 24 units are already expected. With 18 on hand and 24 on an outstanding delivery, another 18 units bring the projected total to 60.

In InventoryOS Retail, outstanding deliveries reduce the reorder calculation. Keep their status current and record partial receipts accurately. If a supplier cancels an order, update it so the system is no longer expecting stock that will not arrive.

Round the shortage into supplier packs

If the supplier sells that water in cases of 12, a shortage of 18 units needs two whole cases, or 24 units. The projected holding becomes 66, so check shelf space and storage before confirming the order.

Configure how many sellable units are received per supplier pack. If one ordered case contains ten products, receiving two cases should increase stock by 20 units. Recording the case as one unit can make both replenishment and cost figures misleading.

Compare cost per unit, not just the case price

A £12 case containing 24 sellable units has a basic product cost of 50p per unit. A £10 case containing 16 units costs 62.5p per unit. The lower case price is not automatically the lower unit cost.

Check the tax basis, delivery charges and pack contents before comparing suppliers. InventoryOS Retail can group reorder quantities by supplier and show estimated costs. Missing costs should be resolved before treating an estimate as an order budget.

Investigate negative stock instead of ignoring it

A negative quantity can indicate an unrecorded delivery, a wrong barcode, an incorrect pack size or another stock-record issue. Count the actual holding and check recent transactions before correcting it. Reordering from an unexplained negative figure can amplify the error.

Retail supports negative stock and flags items for counting. Reviewing count losses separately from damaged or out-of-date waste makes the reason behind a reduction easier to understand.

Review the levels after the shop has traded

Start with a few familiar products and review their actual sales and delivery pattern. Adjust targets for storage limits, shelf life and supplier minimums. A rule that suits a fast-selling ambient product may be unsuitable for fresh goods or a slow seasonal line.
