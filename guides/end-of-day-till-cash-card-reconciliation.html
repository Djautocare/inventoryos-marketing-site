End-of-day till checks: cash, card payments and differences

A useful till close explains what should be in the drawer, what was counted and why the two figures differ. Keep cash movements separate from sales, and compare card records against the terminal that actually processed them.

By the InventoryOS team · 8 November 2026

On this page

  1. Record the starting float and the drawer
  2. Record money moving in and out
  3. Calculate expected cash from recorded movements
  4. Compare card records with the terminal
  5. Resolve pending records before closing
  6. Use differences to find a repeatable improvement

Record the starting float and the drawer

Open the correct drawer with its counted starting float. Staff should use their own login and assigned till so sales and management actions can be traced to the right session. If staff change over, follow your store’s handover routine rather than sharing a PIN.

A float is opening cash, not sales revenue. Keep the opening count available when you close. An unexplained difference at the start of the day should not be hidden inside the day’s takings.

Record money moving in and out

A cash lift removes money from the drawer without being a customer refund. A change delivery adds money without being a sale. Record the movement, amount and reason when it happens, including who made or approved it.

Do not use a stock adjustment to explain missing cash, or a cash lift to explain damaged goods. Wastage changes stock; a cash movement changes the drawer. Distinct records make the closing figures easier to understand.

Calculate expected cash from recorded movements

As a simple example: £100 opening float + £140 cash sales − £20 cash refunds − £50 cash lift + £10 change added = £180 expected in the drawer. This is an illustration, not a report from a real shop.

If the count is £177, the difference is £177 − £180 = −£3. Record the £3 shortage and investigate the cause; do not change the count merely to make the difference disappear. Check that every movement belongs to the same drawer and period.

Compare card records with the terminal

InventoryOS records card payments that staff manually confirm after taking payment on the business’s existing terminal. It does not trigger that terminal, independently verify payment or guarantee settlement. Check the terminal result before confirming the sale.

At close, compare recorded card sales and refunds with the terminal’s matching report and time period. Investigate missing confirmations, duplicate entries or refunds recorded in only one system. Card money is not physical cash and should not be added to the drawer count.

Resolve pending records before closing

Check for unfinished transactions and unsynced back-office cash sales. A local queue or cached page is not proof that a transaction has reached the server. Keep the original device and browser data until the pending records successfully sync.

Restricted staff tills require an internet connection. Complete the store’s closing steps before permanently deleting a store or resetting a device. If a report seems wrong, retain the relevant references and send a support request rather than entering a second sale to compensate.

Use differences to find a repeatable improvement

Review the transaction, refund and cash-movement records around a discrepancy. Repeated small shortages can suggest a process issue; a single large difference may need a recount or a missed cash-lift check. Avoid assuming the staff member caused a loss before reviewing the evidence.
