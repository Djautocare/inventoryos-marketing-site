Resellers · PRACTICAL GUIDE

How to organise stock for online and in-store selling

Selling the same goods in more than one place becomes confusing when each screen holds a separate version of the quantity. Start by deciding where the physical stock is counted and which workflows are allowed to change it.

By the InventoryOS team · 8 November 2026

On this page

  1. Start with the physical goods, not the selling channel
  2. Choose the record that should change after a sale
  3. Reconcile existing counts before linking
  4. Keep raw materials separate from finished products
  5. Turn orders into a controlled dispatch queue
  6. Use the workspace without giving every person every permission

Start with the physical goods, not the selling channel

Write down what you actually hold and where it is. One jacket on a rail is one jacket, even if it has an online listing and could also be sold over the counter. Locations answer “where is it?”; channels answer “where might it sell?”. Treating those as the same thing can produce duplicate counts.

Use separate inventories when the goods really are independent, such as a personal reselling collection and shop stock. A clearer menu does not require combining every business record into one pool.

Choose the record that should change after a sale

For stock shared by supported selling workflows, select the authoritative record and configure the relevant links deliberately. Test a small quantity first. Record a sale and confirm that the expected source reduces once, not once in the till and again as an unrelated manual removal.

Connecting navigation, giving staff access or using the same login does not establish stock synchronisation. Likewise, InventoryOS shipping-label email import is a dispatch workflow; it is not an automatic stock connection to every external marketplace.

Reconcile existing counts before linking

Suppose your inventory screen says five label rolls and your shop screen also says five, but both refer to the same five physical rolls. Adding those balances together would display ten. Before an additive merge, count the goods and decide which records represent distinct stock and which are duplicate records of the same stock.

Review any merge warning and confirm the source and destination. An initial combination must not be repeated as a routine synchronisation step. Keep a record of what you reconciled so future stock differences can be investigated.

Keep raw materials separate from finished products

A maker can hold wax and packaging as materials, then record completed candles as finished products. Creating a production plan is not the same as completing it. Confirm actual production and finished quantities before releasing sellable stock through a supported linked workflow.

Materials, batch costs and traceability still matter after finished goods become available for sale. The materials-planning guide explains how shared recipe ingredients can be consolidated before ordering.

Turn orders into a controlled dispatch queue

A manual order in InventoryOS can record the products and quantities to prepare, then be sent to Dispatch Centre. A draft does not itself take payment or remove stock. Check the right product, location and quantity when you complete the normal packing or removal process.

Forwarded label imports can contain source references and original dates. Reimports should reuse a recognised order rather than become duplicate sales. If a source cannot be matched confidently, review it instead of relying on the timestamp alone. See the batch-label workflow for parcel checks.

Use the workspace without giving every person every permission

The new InventoryOS workspace brings products, orders, dispatch, purchasing, production and reports together. Settings lets you choose which tools appear and switch back to Classic. Those display choices do not alter stock or expand a staff member’s permissions.

Intro covers basic stock for up to 100 active items. Premium adds paid expense and dispatch workflows; Pro adds advanced tools and a first physical store. Compare the plans before enabling features.
